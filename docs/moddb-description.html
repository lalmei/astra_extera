AstraExtera gives every Vintage Story save a sky born with the world. It places an Earth-like home inside a generated galaxy, authors the star that warms it and the system around it, then hands one stored sky to AstraTerra so every player looks up at the same heavens.

The result is not Earth's constellations with different scenery. The density of stars, the Milky Way's shape, the celestial pole, the companion planets, the comets, and even the giant that hangs over a moon world all belong to that save.

⚠ Initial alpha release

Version 0.1.0 is the first public release. Procedural galaxies, stored star catalogs, local systems, giant-planet skies, and the inspection panel are implemented. Procedural deep-sky objects are not yet generated, and a world that is itself a planet keeps Vintage Story's ordinary moon for now.

🧩 Requirements

Vintage Story 1.22.2 or newer and AstraTerra 0.10.0 or newer. Enable AstraTerra and AstraExtera together; AstraExtera replaces the catalogs and nearby bodies that AstraTerra renders.

✓ Contributions welcome

Code, testing, screenshots, translations, astronomy checks, and celestial art are all welcome at the repository and issue tracker.

What is new

🌌 v0.1.0: every world gets its own sky

AstraExtera's first release gives a world an astronomical address before it gives it a sky.

  • A world is placed inside a galaxy. Spiral galaxies are common, giant ellipticals are rare, and the save keeps only sites in a habitable annulus with enough heavy elements to make iron cores and ores. The playable world is an Earth analog with roughly Earth-like radius, gravity, bulk iron, and temperature.
  • The stars follow the place. The visible catalog is sampled from the stellar density and dust extinction around that site. A sparse outer-disk world can have a few thousand naked-eye stars; a crowded inner site can fill the 10,000-star render budget.
  • The galaxy is on the sky. Stars too faint or numerous to resolve remain as a generated galactic glow behind AstraTerra's star billboards, with a world-specific celestial pole deciding how the band wheels overhead.
  • The local system has an architecture. A K-, G-, or F-type host, with M dwarfs permitted when the habitable world is a moon, anchors a liquid-water orbit, inner rocky worlds, a shepherd giant beyond the snow line, and optional outer giants on separated orbits. The comets scattered by the giant and the meteor showers fed by those comets join the sky too.
  • Giants are bodies, not markers. They carry cloud bands, rotation, a long-lived storm, tilted rings with their own composition, and families of moons. Twelve giant textures, sixteen ring systems, and fifty-four moon textures let the rendered face follow the generated body's colour and geometry.
  • A moon world has a moon world's sky. Its parent giant hangs fixed and tens of degrees wide, phases with the sun, and shows its rings edge-on. Sibling moons drift past it and cross its face and rings. Vintage Story's ordinary moon steps aside.
  • Everyone gets the same answer. The server authors the galaxy, star catalog, and local sky once, stores them with the save, and sends them in the join packet. Clients render the stored sky instead of rolling their own.
  • The result is inspectable. Press Ctrl+Shift+S for an in-game panel with face-on and edge-on galaxy figures, an all-sky view, the full local system, and portraits of every companion. /astraextera galaxy reports the same facts in text.

Features

▾  🌌 A place in a generated galaxy.

The world seed first chooses a Milky Way analog and a galactocentric site, not a decorative background. Spiral arms are smooth density waves; rare elliptical galaxies use a habitable spheroid outside their dense core. Metallicity decides whether the site can make the iron core and ores an Earth-like world needs.

The home world then receives its own host star, orbit, radius, gravity, bulk iron fraction, surface temperature, and celestial pole. Those facts are generated once and remain part of the save.

▾  ✨ Stars that belong to that location.

No fixed star count is chosen. The sampler integrates a stellar luminosity function through the local density and accumulating dust extinction, then keeps every star bright enough to see until the 10,000-star catalog budget is full. Brightness-ranked ids are stored with the save, so player-drawn constellations do not scramble when the generator changes later.

The unresolved remainder becomes the galaxy's own glow, drawn behind the stars and correctly hidden by terrain. Earth guide groups, sky cultures, and deep-sky objects are cleared rather than attached to unrelated procedural stars.

▾  🪐 A complete local system.

The habitable orbit sits among up to three inner rocky worlds and a shepherd gas giant beyond the snow line, with an optional second gas giant and one or two ice giants farther out. Every new body must remain Hill-separated from the ones already placed.

Companion planets follow Keplerian tracks around the authored star. Leftover ice becomes returning comets, and each comet leaves a meteor shower where its debris intersects the observer's orbit. These catalogs replace AstraTerra's Earth wanderers instead of being mixed into them.

▾  🌛 Giants with faces, rings, and moons.

A generated giant has an obliquity, rotation period, cloud-band pattern, long-lived storm, equatorial rings, and a family of moons outside those rings. Ring composition — bright ice, rock and dust, or sooted debris — changes the light the planet adds to the sky.

The texture library chooses the supplied giant whose colour best matches the authored cloud decks, then lays the selected ring onto it at the generated tilt, heading, and radius. The far half passes behind the globe, the near half crosses in front, and AstraTerra lights the result from the real sun direction.

▾  🌙 The sky from a habitable moon.

A habitable moon is tidally locked to its giant, so the giant never rises or sets. It hangs in one place, phases with the sun, and can span tens of degrees. Its rings appear almost as a line because the moon formed and travels in the same plane.

Sibling moons drift past the giant according to the difference between their orbit and yours, crossing its face and rings as they go. The ordinary Vintage Story moon is hidden, while the world's day cycle, moonlight, and calendar phase remain untouched.

▾  🖥 A server-authored, inspectable sky.

The server stores the galaxy placement, brightness-ranked star catalog, local-system sky, and nearby bodies. The join packet carries that exact data to each client, which replaces AstraTerra's catalogs only after the server's answer arrives.

Ctrl+Shift+S opens the galaxy panel. Its facts and geometry are shared with the static HTML preview, so the in-game face-on view, edge-on view, all-sky map, full-system figure, and planet portraits cannot quietly disagree with the developer tool.

▾  🛠 Server owners and developers.

Use /astraextera galaxy to inspect the current save's authored site in text. The stored records carry schema versions so old saves can keep their sky as the generator evolves.

For development, make galaxy-preview SEED=42 writes a pinned static HTML preview, while make star-catalog SEED=42 exports the catalog without opening the preview. The mod compiles against a sibling AstraTerra checkout when one is present and otherwise uses the installed dependency.

How I test

Automated tests cover the generated data and code contracts, but they cannot tell me whether the sky looks right or how a collection of mods behaves in-game. I therefore also test manually on a 24 GB M3 MacBook Air with a mostly vanilla-plus set of mods I regularly use: Carry On, Butchering, Better Ruins, Blood Trail, QP Chisel, Buzzwords, Terra Pretty, Watershed, D.E.A.D., Farseer, Footsteps, Food Shelves, Hydrate or Diedrate, RealSmoke, Place On Slabs, Terrain Slabs, Tankards, Vanilla More Molds, Waterfall, and others.

I wish Vintage Story offered an easier way to automate this kind of compatibility testing. If the developers provide one, I intend to add it to this process.

▾  📚 Relationship to AstraTerra.

AstraTerra is the renderer and the observing game: stars, planets, comets, meteor showers, telescopes, the Sextant, the Astrolabe, and player-drawn constellations. AstraExtera is the world author. It replaces AstraTerra's Earth-centered catalogs with the galaxy and system stored in this save.

That separation keeps the generated world deterministic on the server while reusing AstraTerra's instruments and sky engine on every client.
